# The First Principles Thinker
You're asking the wrong question. All five questions assume the problem is "how do we build a better friend-swiping dating app." Strip it down and the actual problem is this: people don't want swiping, they want a good date set up by someone who knows them. Matchmaker-assisted dating already exists in the real world, and it works because of trust and context. The swiping is a UI skin on that.

What the app really sells is the matchmaker-to-matchmaker conversation. Two people who each know their friend well negotiate a date. Everything else (the swipe gestures, voice notes, Hinge-style profiles, the 10-swipe cap) is scaffolding around that one interaction. Ship, Wingman and the others had the swiping and died or stalled. That is evidence the swipe is not the value. The council shouldn't treat the missing chat as a feature gap. It is the product.

Consequences:
1. The core loop is probably wrong. A double-blind mutual swipe needs two matchmakers who each cover two different singles, and each of those singles needs a crew. Per-pair match probability is tiny. A simpler unit exists: one person asks a friend, "will you find me someone?" That is a one-sided referral loop with no cold-start deadlock.
2. Cold start dissolves if the atomic unit is a pair of friends, not a crew. Test with 20 real friend pairs in one university before building any swipe physics. Run it by hand in a group chat. If matchmakers don't enjoy the chat, nothing else matters.
3. The blind date is the real consent problem. The singles never opted into being matched. Fix that before GDPR: the single must opt in to being matchable, or the app is a stranger-brokering machine.
4. MVP is the matchmaker chat plus a minimal candidate card. Cut voice notes, Super Like and the gestures until the chat proves itself.

Build the experiment, not the app.

# The Contrarian
The fatal flaw is the double-yes requirement. A match needs two matchmakers who each hold a candidate and each swipe yes on the other's single. With 10 swipes a day per friend, and a friends-of-friends pool that starts near empty, the chance of a mutual yes is close to zero for months. Users will swipe for a week, see nothing, and leave. Ship died on exactly this, and the app has no fallback for a quiet pool.

Cold start is worse than the framing admits. Every match needs four active people in two linked crews, and both matchmakers have to stay engaged for days. If one matchmaker goes quiet, the match and the date both stall. Nobody owns the stall, and nobody gets told about it.

The consent model is the next problem. People are put in front of strangers, and then in front of the strangers' friends, without ever agreeing to it. A friend adds you, and suddenly your photos, voice notes and orientation sit in a third party's swipe deck. Under GDPR that is processing special-category data (sexual orientation) with no direct consent from the data subject. "Not told about a match" also means that when a date request arrives, the single learns that two people they trust were secretly pairing them off. That will feel creepy to some people, not cozy.

The blind date adds physical risk. Two matchmakers who have never met approve a time and place for two singles who haven't consented to the specifics. There is no identity verification. Fake profiles and "friends" of fake accounts will flood the city pool. Minors are a real risk too, and you have no age gating.

Voice notes mean storing and moderating audio on Supabase's free tier. That will hit the storage limits quickly, and nobody is set up to moderate it.

Super Like is not a business. It gives matchmakers a way to push someone's friend forward, so it will be spammed or resented, and it adds nothing to the part that is actually broken.

Cut the city pool, the voice notes and the Super Like. Prove that one tiny friend-group loop works first.

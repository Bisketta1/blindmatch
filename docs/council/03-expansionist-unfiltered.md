# The Expansionist (unfiltered, free rein)
Core insight: the app's real asset is not the swipe. It is a consented social graph of who vouches for whom, plus a two-sided trust channel between matchmakers. Dating is the first use. "Friends act on behalf of friends, blind, with a human in the loop" is a general primitive. It also solves cold start differently: the matchmaker is the growth engine, and the single is just the beneficiary.

Ideas, ranked by how big they could get:
1. Blindmatch as a "friend-brokered introduction" network (the primitive, not the app). Biggest ceiling. The matchmaker-to-matchmaker chat plus double-approval plus blind reveal is a vouching protocol. Dating is the wedge. The same rails work for friend-making for people who just moved, mentor intros, co-founder matching, roommate matching, "find my friend a band." Rename the category from "dating app" to "introductions app that happens to start with romance." The graph is the moat.
2. City-wide Blind Date Nights (event layer). Once a city has enough matched pairs, run one night a month: "Blindmatch Night." Venues host date slots, all approved dates cluster into one night. The city lights up in the app, people post "my matchmakers set me up." Local press, TikTok content, a reason to open the app at the same time.
3. Venue-funded dates (monetization that costs the user nothing). Venues pay per redeemed date: a free dessert or two-for-one coffee when the date code is shown. Matchmakers pick from "cozy partner spots" with a perk. Sponsored "Date Menu" packs (picnic kit from a florist, board-game cafe night). Revenue per date could dwarf Super Like revenue and is aligned with dates actually happening.
4. The Matchmaker as creator/identity. Public optional "Matchmaker Card": matches made, dates that happened, couples still together after 6 months. Titles like "Cupid Level 3." Leaderboards per friend group or campus. Couples can later thank both matchmakers: a viral share asset.
5. AI Matchmaker Copilot. Drafts the opening message in the matchmaker chat, suggests date ideas fitting both profiles, writes the "blind teaser" the singles receive. Later a "solo mode" where an AI is your matchmaker if you have no friends on the app yet, which solves cold start for singles, who then invite real friends as an upgrade.
6. Group dates, double dates, "Crew Nights." Four-person blind dates: two singles plus both matchmakers, which kills most safety and awkwardness concerns and is hugely shareable. Could be the default first-date format in new markets.
7. B2B: universities, companies, expat communities. Campus ambassador program, branded "Freshers' Matchmaking Week"; cross-company "Friday matchmaker" wellness perk; expat/diaspora groups with dense trusted friend graphs and matchmaking traditions (shaadi aunties, shidduch, omiai), "Aunties Mode".
8. Gift and Super Like economy. Matchmakers gift a friend a "boost", singles thank matchmakers with tips or a gift card, a pooled "Matchmaker Fund". Monetization as gifting between friends feels warm, and each gift pulls both parties back in.
9. Data/social-graph value (long game). Vouched, friend-verified relationship data enables adjacent products (rebounds, long-distance, post-breakup). Do not sell it.
10. International and language packs. Translation-assisted matchmaker chat for cross-border diaspora matches.

Favorites: A. The primitive play (#1) with a romance wedge: architecture treats "introduction" as a generic object, costs nothing now. B. Venue-funded dates (#3) + monthly Blind Date Night (#2): growth event, revenue without a paywall, shareable content. C. Double-date / four-person blind formats (#6) with the AI Copilot (#5): safer, funnier, more viral, and lowers the matchmaker effort that is the weakest link.
First markets: one university town with dense student scene (5-10 societies and a Freshers' event) or one diaspora community in one city.
